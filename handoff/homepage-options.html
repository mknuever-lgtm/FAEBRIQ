What it does well

  • A product is visible immediately. Matches what the conversion data says apparel homepages should do.
  • Reads as a shop. A cold visitor from TikTok knows in one second that something is for sale.

What it costs

  • It puts your weakest asset first. At phone width the hero product fills most of the screen. A flat pasted-on print gets bigger, not better.

  • Needs the mockup fix before it is worth shipping.

My call

Option A now. Option B the week your mockups are fixed.

The research says put a product in the hero, and it is right. But it also says real photography beats renders by 2.4x, and your renders are the thing you keep flagging. Option B takes your weakest asset and makes it the largest thing on the screen.

Option A ships today, wins most of the same ground through the announcement bar, trust row and curated grid, and keeps the restraint that actually makes FÆBRIQ look unlike anything else in this niche. Then you swap in Option B once there is a photograph worth putting at that size.

On the mockups: product tiles here are drawn in CSS, not photographs. That is deliberate. This page is a decision about layout, and dropping in the current renders would bias the comparison toward whichever one hid them better.
