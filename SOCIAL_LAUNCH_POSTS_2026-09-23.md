## LinkedIn (founder posts, from Maurice's profile)

### LI 1. The quiet calculation
> Every queer person in tech runs a quiet calculation before a new client call.
>
> How visible? For whom? At what cost?
>
> Nobody teaches it. Everybody does it.
>
> Rainbow lanyard at a Pride tech meetup: easy.
> Rainbow lanyard in a room of prospects you haven't read yet: less easy.
> Plain black tee: safe, and a little like disappearing.
>
> That gap is why I built FÆBRIQ.
>
> The design rule is strict. Every piece carries one statement in serif caps, one smaller line underneath, and one flat six-block stripe.
>
> To most of the room, it reads as good graphic design.
> To the right people, it reads as a handshake.
>
> No speech. No disclosure event. No explaining yourself before the coffee arrives.
>
> Signal, not volume.
>
> The store is live at faebriq.com and ships to the US and Canada.
>
> Question for the queer people in tech here: what's your version of the quiet calculation? A pin, a lanyard, a laptop sticker, or nothing at all?
>
> #QueerInTech #LGBTQ

### LI 2. A merch brand on a Chromebook
> A Chromebook and an Android phone. That's the full hardware stack behind FÆBRIQ.
>
> No studio. No warehouse. No inventory.
>
> Five things building a merch brand this way taught me:
>
> 1. Print-on-demand kills inventory risk, not work. Each order gets printed when it's placed. Zero unsold boxes. You still own every photo, title, and size chart.
>
> 2. Your tools will overwrite each other. Publishing from Printify to Shopify wipes manual edits: titles, alt text, image order. I edit in one place now.
>
> 3. Generated mockups will misspell your own name. Mine garbled the wordmark and showed the wrong design. Every image gets a human check before it goes live.
>
> 4. Put shipping in the price. A surprise fee at checkout is where carts go to die.
>
> 5. Launch beats polish. The store is live while half the product photos get reshot. Waiting for perfect is a cost that never sends an invoice.
>
> Cost to test the idea: a Shopify plan and a lot of evenings.
> Cost of not testing it: never knowing.
>
> Building something solo on a tight budget? Which tool fought you hardest?
>
> #BuildInPublic #Ecommerce #SoloFounder
